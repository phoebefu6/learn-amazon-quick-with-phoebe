What Quick actually is

Amazon Quick is AWS's answer to a question every data team now hears: can our people just ask? It bundles dashboards, chat over documents, chat over data, a research agent and workflow automation into one product with one per-user price. Tonight we trace where it came from, take it apart into the pieces you will actually configure, read the plans for who can build what, and stand up a first space with a chat agent on the free trial.

Part 0

Where we start

The running case is constructed. Oakmere Insurance is an invented regional insurer in Singapore. Over two years its data team built Compass, an in-house knowledge platform inside Oakmere's own AWS account: documents and claims data in an S3 lake, a Glue catalog, Lake Formation rules that hide personal data from anyone without clearance, a chatbot that answers from policy documents, a text-to-SQL assistant over Athena, and an MCP server that lets Oakmere's AI agents query all of it. The AWS account team has now offered Amazon Quick. Priya, Oakmere's head of data (constructed), has three sessions to decide whether Quick replaces Compass, sits on top of it, or stays out.

Part 1 · covers the lineage

Two products and a new layer

5 min live

Quick is not a new product so much as a merger with a new layer on top. Amazon QuickSight was AWS's BI tool. Amazon Q Business was its chat-over-company-documents service. In October 2025 AWS put both under one name, Amazon Quick Suite, and added agents that research, run workflows and take actions. Then it kept moving.

LiveWhat "merger" means for a buyer3 min▶

If your organisation already pays for QuickSight, you are partly a Quick customer today. AWS maps old QuickSight Reader licences to the new Essentials plan and Author licences to Enterprise (until 31 March 2027). If you built on Q Business, you face a forced move: AWS closed it to new customers at the end of July 2026 (its own pages give the 30th and the 31st), keeps it in maintenance with no new features, and offers a route called Bring Your Own Index that plugs at most two Q Business indexes per Region into Quick. Q Apps, actions and guardrails do not carry over.

For Oakmere this matters less: Compass replaced the need for Q Business. But Priya notes the pattern. A managed service that closed one product to new customers within two years of launching its successor can do it again.

Self-studyWhich name to use2 min▶

AWS pages today say both "AWS Quick" and "Amazon Quick", and older blogs say "Amazon Quick Suite". The documentation now lives under docs.aws.amazon.com/quick/. Search with all three names: posts from the first months still use Quick Suite, and the BI part keeps some QuickSight URLs.

Part 2 · covers the parts

Seven parts, and the Compass piece each one competes with

6 min live

Strip the marketing and Quick is a stack. At the bottom sit the things it reads: datasets and connected knowledge. In the middle, the ways people ask: chat agents, Quick Sight questions and the research agent. On top, the things that act: Flows and Automate. Beside it runs one door outward, actions and MCP, that lets Quick call other systems.

LiveThe reading layer: Index, Spaces, datasets3 min▶

Quick Index and knowledge bases pull documents in through connectors: S3, SharePoint, OneDrive, Google Drive, Confluence and a web crawler among them. Sync runs daily by default, and one sync run stops after 14 days. The index grows automatically up to 2 TB per account.

Spaces are shared team areas that hold files, knowledge, data and agents together: up to 10,000 files and under 10 GB each, with single uploads up to 500 MB for PDF, Word and PowerPoint and 50 MB for other types.

Datasets belong to the Quick Sight side: tables from Athena, Redshift, Aurora PostgreSQL and others, either queried directly or copied into SPICE, Quick's in-memory store. Hold on to the word SPICE: session 2 shows it matters for who can see what.

LiveThe asking layer: agents, Sight, Research2 min▶

Chat agents answer from the knowledge, data and actions you attach to them, with citations. You can build custom agents for a team. There is no model picker: you choose a speed mode (AWS's pages say Fast, Balanced and Smart), not a model.

Quick Sight is the BI part: dashboards plus natural-language questions. Since 4 May 2026 its Dataset Q&A writes SQL in the right dialect for Athena, Redshift, Aurora PostgreSQL, Iceberg tables in S3 table buckets and SPICE, and shows that SQL on request.

Quick Research is a deep-research agent that writes cited reports from internal data, the web and third-party datasets.

Self-studyThe acting layer and the one door out4 min▶

Flows are workflows written in plain language; since December 2025 a Flow can run Research as a step. Automate, on Enterprise only, runs multi-step work across systems with a human approval step.

Actions and MCP. Quick ships action connectors (Salesforce, Jira, ServiceNow, Slack, email and more) and can connect to remote MCP servers, turning their tools into actions. The direction matters: Quick is an MCP client. It can call Compass's MCP server; Oakmere's own AI agents have no documented way to call Quick. On Enterprise only, remote servers only, up to 100 tools per server.

Real world

The demo that always lands. The usual vendor demo connects SharePoint, asks a policy question, gets a cited answer, then turns the answer into a scheduled report with one sentence. Every step is real. What a demo never shows is the permission model on the documents, the region the prompt was processed in, or the bill in month four. Sessions 2 and 3 are those three questions.

Part 3 · covers the plans

Who can build what

4 min live

Quick is priced per person, and the plan decides what that person can do, not just how much they can use. The single most expensive surprise is that the controls an insurer needs sit on the top plan. Session 2 prices it; here, read the table for capability.

PlanPer user / monthCan doCannot do
Essentials$3Read dashboards, light chat (1 agent hour; 3 until 31 Jan 2027)Build anything
Professional$20Chat agents, Research, Flows, 8 agent hours, 25 GB pooled indexDashboards, Quick Sight datasets, Automate
Enterprise$40Everything above, plus dashboards, Automate, MCP, customer keys, private network, 18 agent hours, 50 GBPick the model
Individual Free / Plus / Max$0 / $20-25 / $100-125No AWS account, no account fee; Plus up to 300 usersOrganisation controls
LiveRead the plan for the controls, not the usage2 min▶

A plan comparison invites you to ask "how heavy is each user?". For a regulated buyer the first question is different: which controls does our risk team require, and which plan carries them? Customer-managed encryption keys, a private network path to data sources, MCP and the switch that keeps new AI features off until an admin turns them on are all Enterprise. Whether those controls cover Professional users in the same account is not stated on any public AWS page. Until AWS answers that in writing, plan on $40 a head.

Self-studyThe individual plans are a different product2 min▶

Free, Plus and Max need no AWS account and pay no account fee. They suit a person or a small team trying Quick on their own files. They are not a way to give a regulated company cheap seats: none of the organisation controls above come with them, and the data sits outside your AWS organisation's governance.

Build-along

Your first space and chat agent, on the trial

★ 22 min · everyone builds

The trial gives 30 days of Enterprise for up to 25 users, with the account fee waived and no card needed. Priya's analyst does exactly what you will do: one space, three documents, three questions, one agent, and a note of everything that surprised them. Use documents you are allowed to upload: public ones are safest for a first run.

Start the trial and write down two things. From the Quick console, start the 30-day Enterprise trial. Record the AWS Region it runs in and the date the trial ends: billing for Enterprise starts automatically afterwards. If the console offers a choice of identity (IAM Identity Center or another), pick Identity Center for anything beyond a test; AWS's guidance says switching away from it later means ending the subscription.

★ Step 1 · your trial cardRegion: ap-southeast-1 (Singapore) Trial ends: ____ (calendar reminder set: yes / no) Identity: IAM Identity Center Users invited: just me, for now

Create a space with a narrow job. A space for "everything" teaches you nothing. Oakmere's analyst names theirs after one question the claims team asks every week. Keep the name and description plain; the agent you build in step 5 will lean on them.

★ Step 2 · the spaceName: Motor claims policy questions Description: What our motor policy wording covers and excludes. Answers must cite the clause.

Upload three documents. Use public documents for the first run: a published annual report, a product brochure and a public policy wording are ideal. PDF, Word and PowerPoint up to 500 MB each. Watch how long indexing takes and whether every file shows as ready.

Ask three questions and grade each answer. One the documents answer directly, one that needs two documents together, and one the documents cannot answer at all. The third is the real test: a good assistant says it does not know; a weak one invents.

★ Step 4 · the three questionsQ1 What does the policy say about windscreen damage? cited? __ right clause? __ Q2 Which product in the brochure matches the cover in section 4 of the wording? cited both? __ Q3 What was our claims ratio last March? (not in any document) said "not found"? __ invented? __

Build a chat agent scoped to the space. Give it the space as its only knowledge, a one-line role, and one instruction about citations. Ask it Q3 again. Then share it with one colleague and ask them to try to make it answer something outside the space.

★ Step 5 · the agentName: Motor wording helper Knowledge: space "Motor claims policy questions" only Instructions: Answer only from the attached documents. Quote the clause number. If the documents do not answer the question, say so and stop.

Write the surprise list. Three lines: what was easier than you expected, what was harder, and one thing you would need to ask AWS before letting real Oakmere documents in. Bring it to session 2.

Menus move. Quick's console changes with each release, and button names drift. The concepts in these steps, space, knowledge, agent, instructions, are stable; if a label differs, the Spaces guide ↗ is the reference.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 1 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Oakmere's AI agents need to query the documents and data Quick has indexed. What does the documentation support?

Quick's MCP support runs one way: it connects to remote MCP servers and uses their tools. No AWS page describes outside agents calling Quick. For Oakmere that means Compass's MCP server keeps serving agents whatever is decided.

2 · Priya's risk team requires customer-managed encryption keys. Which plan must Oakmere budget for?

Customer-managed keys are listed as an Enterprise feature. Whether they extend to Professional users in a mixed account is not stated publicly, so the safe budget is $40 a head until AWS says otherwise.

3 · In the build-along, why ask a question the documents cannot answer?

Answering from the documents is the easy case. An assistant that fills a gap with a plausible number does damage precisely because it sounds right. AWS states Quick does not train on customer data, so D is wrong on the facts too.
