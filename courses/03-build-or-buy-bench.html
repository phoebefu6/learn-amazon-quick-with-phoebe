Part 1 · covers lock-in

What leaves with you

5 min live

Lock-in is not a feeling. It is a list of the things you built inside a product, sorted by whether they can be exported in a form something else can read. For Quick the list splits cleanly: the BI half travels, the agentic half, as far as public documentation shows, does not.

LiveThree quieter forms of lock-in3 min▶

The model. Chat offers speed modes, not models. Oakmere cannot pin a model version for an audited process, or move to a cheaper model when one appears. Only Automate's custom agents can bring a chosen Bedrock model, billed separately.

The identity choice. AWS's guidance says moving off IAM Identity Center once chosen means ending the Quick subscription. Choose it deliberately on day one.

The direction of MCP. Quick consumes MCP servers. A company whose agents depend on Quick for knowledge has nothing to point them at; a company whose agents use its own MCP server can swap the front end at any time. That asymmetry is the strongest argument for keeping Compass's MCP layer whatever else changes.

Self-studyA disagreement worth testing yourself2 min▶

How long Quick waits for an MCP tool before giving up is reported two ways. AWS's MCP page says a fixed five minutes. An independent migration guide (ecorpit, November 2026) says 60 seconds, and also that tool lists stay fixed after registration, where current AWS docs describe a manual Sync. The sources may describe different dates or different connector types. If Compass's slow queries would sit near either limit, test it on the trial rather than picking a side.

Part 2 · covers total cost

The line people forget

5 min live

The finance director compared Quick's invoice with Compass's AWS bill. That sum leaves out the largest line on the in-house side: the people who build and run it. Quick's price includes AWS's people. Compass's AWS bill does not include Oakmere's.

LiveSeats price people; in-house prices questions3 min▶

Quick charges for every person who can use it, whether they ask one question a month or five hundred. An in-house platform charges for the questions asked (model tokens, Athena scans) on top of a mostly fixed base. So Quick favours a small group of regular users; in-house favours a large group of occasional users, and it is the only option when the main "users" are AI agents, which hold no seat at all.

That is why the bench scales head count while holding the tier mix and per-person habits steady, and why its fifth rung adds agent queries Quick cannot serve.

Self-studyWhere to get your own in-house numbers3 min▶

Run cost: AWS Cost Explorer filtered by the platform's cost-allocation tags gives Fargate, load balancer, RDS and vector store spend. Usage: divide last month's Bedrock and Athena spend by questions answered. Team: the share of each engineer's time spent on the platform, times a fully loaded monthly cost. Independent write-ups note an OpenSearch Serverless vector store has a floor of roughly $350 a month, which S3 Vectors lowers; treat that as a starting point to check, not a quote.

Part 3 · covers the decision

Three options, not two

5 min live

"Build or buy" hides the option most companies end up with. Quick can call an MCP server, and Compass has one. So Oakmere can keep the governed layer it built and let Quick sit on top as one more front end, for the people who want answers inside Slack, Microsoft 365 or the browser.

LiveThe hybrid's one catch2 min▶

In the hybrid, Compass applies Lake Formation before it answers, so a clearance-2 user asking through Quick still never sees a customer's identity number. But whatever Compass returns enters Quick, and from there it is processed under Quick's rules: in Singapore, potentially in any commercial Region. The hybrid moves the governance problem from "every row in the lake" to "every answer Compass hands over". That is a much smaller surface, and it is still a surface. Oakmere's risk team decides whether the answers Compass returns may leave the Region in flight.

    Check yourself

    Three questions

    Clear all three and session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

    1 · The finance director compares Quick's invoice ($5,850) with Compass's AWS bill ($4,700) and concludes Compass is cheaper. What is wrong?

    With 1.5 engineers at the constructed $12,000 a month, Compass costs $22,700, not $4,700. Leaving out people is the most common error in build-or-buy sums. Individual plans lack the organisation controls Oakmere needs.

    2 · In rung 5, Oakmere's agents make 200,000 queries a month. Why does Quick's bill not change?

    Quick is an MCP client, not a server, and agents hold no seat. The queries land on the in-house side by necessity, which is why agent-heavy plans point to keeping the governed layer.

    3 · In the hybrid, what still needs the risk team's decision?

    Compass still applies Lake Formation before answering, and agents keep its MCP server. But what Compass hands to Quick is then processed under Quick's routing, which in Singapore is global. The surface is smaller, not gone.
